# NIKAH-001 — Market Research

**Status:** Completed — Preliminary  
**Evidence Level:** Directional / Requires validation  
**Market:** Bangladesh

## Research Question

Is there a commercially viable opportunity for a premium, purpose-built Nikah gifting experience in Bangladesh?

## Evidence Summary

The existing research indicates:

- Bangladesh has an established gifting ecosystem.
- Personalized gifting is an established and growing market behavior.
- Online and social channels are important for gift discovery and purchase.
- Curated gift boxes and premium hampers already exist.
- Wedding gifting is a recognized commercial occasion.
- Existing competitors demonstrate willingness to transact at multi-thousand-taka price points.

## Emerging Opportunity

The research suggests a possible position between generic gift boxes and broad luxury wedding gifting:

> **A highly curated, purpose-built Nikah gifting experience combining meaning, personalization, premium products, keepsake value, and refined presentation.**

This is a **hypothesis, not a confirmed market gap**.

## What Is Known

1. Gifting demand exists.
2. Wedding gifting exists.
3. Premium gifting exists.
4. Personalization is common.
5. Curated hampers are commercially established.
6. Digital ordering is viable.

## What Is Not Yet Proven

- Demand for LAXEE's exact proposition.
- Willingness to pay for the proposed product.
- Optimal product composition.
- Optimal price and margin.
- Size of the premium Nikah segment.
- Strength of diaspora demand.
- Whether the proposed differentiation is strong enough to win.

## Risks

- Price sensitivity.
- Established competitors.
- Easy imitation of generic gift-box concepts.
- Supplier inconsistency.
- Packaging cost.
- Margin pressure.
- Market evidence being broader than product-specific evidence.

## Decision

**MARKET: PROMISING — CONTINUE VALIDATION**

Do not treat market-level evidence as proof of product-market fit.

## Next Evidence Needed

Customer validation → Product validation → Supplier validation → Unit economics → Prototype testing.
